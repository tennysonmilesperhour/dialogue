# IDENTITY.md

## Name

**dialogue**, lowercase in the wordmark. Lowercase reads as deliberate and matches the app's posture: it talks with you, never at you.

The name carries the architecture. The gate is the opening line. The debrief is the closing line. The weekly review is the conversation you have been having with yourself all along.

## Voice

dialogue is a good bookkeeper. Precise, unsentimental, never moralizing. It states what happened and lets you draw the conclusion.

**It sounds like:**
- "Most of your No verdicts land after 10pm."
- "Bored sessions average 22 minutes. Reply sessions average 3."
- "Gate is lighter this week. Your match rate hit 88%."
- "Honest answers welcome. Bored is a legal entry."

**It never sounds like:**

<!-- copy-lint: off, these are the counter-examples -->
- "You've wasted 4 hours this week!" (shame)
- "Let's crush your goals!" (hype)
- "Take a mindful breath." (wellness cliché)
- "Are you sure you want to continue?" (nagging, and implies a wrong answer)
<!-- copy-lint: on -->

Rules: no exclamation points. No emoji in product copy. No second-person accusations. Numbers do the persuading. Never use em dashes; use commas, parentheses, or rewrite.

## Design language

**Analog ledger, not wellness app.** Every competitor is soft gradients, rounded blobs, and calming blues. dialogue is ink on paper: an accountant's book, a captain's log.

- **Paper:** warm cream (#F5F1E6), full-width ruled lines, single red margin rule down the left at 26px.
- **Ink:** near-black (#1C1A16) for entries. Bookkeeping green (#2E5E3F) for kept intentions. Ledger red (#B23A2F) for the margin, reminders, and broken ones.
- **Borders:** hard 1.5px black rules with offset shadows. No soft blur. The weight is the point.
- **Type:** Fraunces for display and app names. IBM Plex Mono for every number, label, and timestamp, tabular figures always. IBM Plex Serif for body and the user's own written reminders, italic.
- **Stamps:** verdicts land as rubber stamps, rotated a few degrees, multiply blend. "Dismissed +1" in red, "Logged" in green. This is the app's one moment of delight and it should feel physical.
- **Named columns:** during setup the user names each watched app ("What do you call this app?"). This is load-bearing, not flavor: Apple's tokens expose no app names to us, so the user's own labels are the only names that can print in the debrief notification, the weekly review, and the ledger. Set them in Plex Serif italic, like the reminder lines. The user's handwriting in the book.

**Direction locked:** the heavier analog v1, not the sleek iOS-native pass. The ink weight is what makes it not look like every other app in the category, and it sets the design for the Shield screens.

## The Shield screen is the product

Most users will see the gate card far more than the app itself. It is the first impression, the daily impression, and the screenshot people share. Design it first, design it best, and never let it grow past one card of content.

## Positioning line

> Every screen time app tells you how long. dialogue tells you whether you meant it.

## App Store presentation

- **Subtitle:** "Did you mean to open that?"
- **Category:** Health & Fitness, secondary Productivity. Avoid the parental-control framing entirely.
- **Screenshot order:** gate card first (it is the hook), then debrief with stamp, then weekly review with the reason cost table, then home, then the adaptive gate explainer.
- The reason cost table ("Bored: avg 22 min / Reply: avg 3 min") is the single most shareable artifact in the product. Build marketing around it.
